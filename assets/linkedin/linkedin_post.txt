The SOC Home Lab Handbook. Free. Public. Built to be attacked and defended — safely.

Every other NESHBOY volume assumes a SIEM, telemetry, and alerts already exist. This is the book for the reader who has none of that yet: a SIEM to search, Linux and Windows endpoints producing real logs, a segmented network, Zeek and Suricata on the wire, honeypots pulling in unsolicited attacker traffic, threat-intel enrichment, dashboards worth looking at, and safe, reversible attack simulation to generate known-cause telemetry on demand.

249 pages. ~95,800 words. 22 parts. And 22 mandatory Safety Gates — one in every single part, no exceptions — because half of what this book teaches you to build is, by design, meant to be attacked.

It's also honest about where the real evidence stops. The author's own lab is real, continuously running, and Linux-and-network only — a Pi-hole resolver, a honeynet, several monitored containers. That's where the REAL LAB EXAMPLE evidence actually comes from. No Windows host, no Active Directory domain, no cloud tenant — and the book says so plainly instead of faking it.

Which is exactly what Part 11 is for. The author tried a persistent two-DC Active Directory forest for Kerberos telemetry practice. It never finished. GPO rollout silently failed to apply — no error, just an endpoint quietly keeping its old audit policy — and the build stalled at a half-configured single DC. Part 11 is that build's autopsy: what a domain lab actually costs, why an auditd rule failing is nothing like a GPO failing, and the realistic scoped-down alternative that doesn't turn a hobbyist into an unpaid part-time AD administrator.

That's the standard for the whole book: build it for real when it's real, label it CONCEPTUAL or OFFICIAL REFERENCE when it isn't, and never blur the two together.
